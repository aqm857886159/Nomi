Ask AI to generate music or a sound when the edit needs something unique. When Library already contains a suitable sound, preview and drag it to the timeline instead. Audio from either path can be moved, trimmed, and mixed like a regular clip.
First check
Library → Sound Effects
for a suitable supplied sound. Generate only when you need something unique.
For music, decide the mood, genre, instruments, tempo, target duration, and whether vocals are allowed.
For sound effects, decide the source, action, environment, intensity, listening distance, and duration.
To synchronize with the picture, plan to use
@
for a clip or exact time. You may also prepare a rhythm or style reference without asking to copy a protected work.
Prompt
Copy
Generate 30 seconds of instrumental background music for
@product-demo
: modern electronic, steady rhythm, positive but not overexcited. Fade in at 00:04 and leave a natural fade at the end.
Create an eight-second audio logo for
@interview-intro
using warm piano and a soft synthesizer, with no drums. Hold the final chord for one second so it can lead into dialogue.
Generate and add a 0.8-second close, metallic latch sound at the action in
@door-shot
. Make it clean and slightly heavy, without environmental reverb.
Use
to identify the clip, range, or action point the audio should support.
Enter the music or sound description and send it. Check the estimate if a confirmation appears.
Follow progress in
My Assets
.
Drag the completed audio to the target audio track. If the prompt specified placement, ChatCut can place it on the timeline directly.
Select the audio clip to adjust its start, length, and volume on the timeline and in the Inspector.

in the media panel.
Search or select a card to preview the sound.
Drag it to the target time on an audio track.
It becomes a regular audio clip that you can move, trim, and mix.
AI music and sound generation use credits. Supplied Library sounds do not need to be generated again. Use the estimate before generation and the completed entry under
Credits History → Detail
as the source of truth.
