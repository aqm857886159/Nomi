AI Voiceover turns a script into narration. Use a voice from Voice Library or a custom voice saved under
My Voices
. The result is a project audio asset; whether it is placed on the timeline depends on whether your prompt specifies a destination.
First decide whether to generate AI narration or record your own voice. AI narration needs a final or near-final script.
Choose a voice from
Voice Library
or
. If none fits, clone a voice you are authorized to use first.
Decide the language, tone, pace, pronunciation, and any clip, time range, or narration the result must match.
If you only want to record narration, no prompt is needed; use the Timeline recording control directly.
Prompt
Copy
Read the following script in Mandarin with the attached voice. Sound natural and trustworthy, with a slightly slower pace. Place the result on A1 starting at 00:03:
[paste script]
Create an English voiceover for
@product-demo
, about 20 seconds long, concise and energetic. Match each sentence to the current visuals and add the result to an available audio track.
Regenerate
@existing-voiceover
with
@my-voice
. Only correct the pronunciation of “Start creating now”; keep the rest of the script, pauses, and timeline position unchanged.
For timing-sensitive narration, state a start time, target duration, or the clip it must match. Avoid a prompt that only says “generate a voiceover.”
Select
+ → Voices
below the AI composer.
Preview voices under
Featured
. Click
Browse all voices
for search, filters, and voice cloning — see
.
Click a voice name or
Use
on its card. This attaches the voice to the draft but does not generate audio yet.
Add the script and placement instructions, then send. The generated audio appears in
My Assets
If it was not placed automatically, drag it from
to A1, A2, or another audio track.
Click the arrow beside the recording control above the timeline and choose
Voiceover
. Confirm the microphone and countdown, then start recording. When you stop, ChatCut saves the recording and, depending on the entry point, adds it to the project or places it at the playhead.
AI narration uses credits; selecting a voice alone does not generate audio. Review pronunciation, pauses, volume, and sync, then move, trim, or replace the audio on the timeline.
