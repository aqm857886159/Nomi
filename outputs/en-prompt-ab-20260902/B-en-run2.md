【Overall Form】
The lamp is a first-order Fresnel lens assembly, a towering beehive-shaped cage of brass and glass. The central Argand-style oil burner sits at the heart of the lens, a brass cylinder with four concentric wicks arranged around a central air tube, the fuel reservoir below. Surrounding the burner, the Fresnel lens is composed of concentric rings of curved glass prisms held in a brass frame, the prisms arranged in vertical panels that can be opened on hinges for servicing. The lens floats on a mercury bath bearing that allows it to rotate, driven by a clockwork mechanism in the base. The matchbox is a simple wooden slide-box, the exterior covered in printed paper with a manufacturer's label, the interior containing a row of wooden matches with red phosphorus tips. Beside the lamp, a brass service ledge holds the matchbox and a small tin of spare wicks.

【Material Composition】
Primary (lens frame): Solid brass, cast and machined, polished to a dull gleam on the interior surfaces and tarnished to a dark brown with green oxidation on the exterior, the brass warm-toned and heavy. The frame is riveted and bolted, the fasteners also brass, their slots showing the marks of a screwdriver.
Secondary (prisms): Crown glass, each prism individually ground and polished, the glass thick and heavy, slightly green-tinted at the edges, the surfaces microscopically scratched from decades of cleaning with salt-stiffened cloths. The prisms are set into the brass frame with thin copper shims.
Tertiary (burner): The Argand burner is brass, its base threaded into the oil supply line, the four wicks made of woven cotton, their tops charred black from previous use. The oil reservoir is a copper tank, its seams soldered, the exterior wrapped in felt insulation.
Inlays (matchbox): The matchbox label is printed in faded black and red ink on yellowed paper, the text reading "SAFETY MATCHES" above a ship in a bottle, the paper edge worn and lifting at the corners.

【Craft and Traces of Age】
The Fresnel lens was manufactured by Barbier & Fenestre in Paris, circa 1880, each prism hand-ground and tested. The brass frame shows the file marks of hand-fitting at the joints. Use marks include soot blackening on the interior of the lens panels above the burner, oil residue pooling in the brass drip tray beneath the burner, and a fine coating of salt haze on the exterior glass from sea spray that has seeped through the lantern room vents. The clockwork mechanism in the base, though not visible, has worn gears that tick with a slight irregularity. The matchbox is worn at the corners, the slide action loosened from use, the striker strip on the side darkened with phosphorus residue. The matches inside are slightly warped from humidity, their tips a dull red-brown.

【Ornament and Patterning】
The Fresnel lens is itself a functional ornament, its concentric prism rings creating a jewel-like geometry when viewed from any angle. The brass frame carries a small engraved plaque on the service door: "BARBIER & FENESTRE / CONSTRUCTEURS / PARIS / No. 1847." The matchbox label features a lithographed illustration of a three-masted ship inside a bottle, framed by ropework borders, the ink faded to sepia.

【Functional Detail】
The lamp operates by drawing oil up through the four wicks via capillary action, the flame burning at the focal point of the Fresnel lens, which refracts and concentrates the light into a horizontal beam visible for 20 miles at sea. The clockwork rotation mechanism causes the lens to turn at a fixed rate, creating the lighthouse's characteristic flashing pattern. The service door on the lens panel hinges open to allow the keeper to trim wicks and refill oil. The matchbox slides open to reveal the matches, and the striker strip on the side ignites the phosphorus tip when the match is drawn across it.

【Special Conditions】
The lamp is cold and dark at the start of the scene. The wicks have burned down to charred nubs. The oil reservoir is half-full, the spermaceti oil congealed slightly in the cold. Maren's match is the first light to enter the lens in over twelve hours. When the lamp catches, the flame surges upward and the prisms flare to life, the beam punching through the dawn haze.
